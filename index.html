What I Bring to the Prosthetic Foot Project

A cost-effective prosthetic foot sits exactly at the intersection of the two things I've spent the last few years doing: designing load-bearing assistive hardware, and analyzing how people actually move through it.

Biomechanical design & problem-solving

At QBMET, I designed and stress-tested the knee and hip components of an exoskeleton that had to survive real gait loading — aluminum, plastic, and composite parts, validated with FEA and physical testing, not just simulation. I know what it takes to take an assistive device from a SolidWorks model to something that holds up under a body's weight and motion.

Internship research experience

At Has-Motion, I spent a year processing gait, golf, and baseball motion capture and force data, and applying SPM, PCA, and ANOVA to pull real signal out of noisy human movement data. For a prosthetic foot, that means I can help the team validate designs against actual gait data, not just intuition — and communicate those results clearly, the way I did presenting at the Ontario Biomechanics Conference.
